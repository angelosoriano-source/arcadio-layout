# What is Arcadio?
Arcadio is a keyboard layout designed to pragmatically and efficiently solve the most frustrating biomechanical flaws when typing in Spanish.

# What changes compared to QWERTY?
Arcadio keeps 23 out of 27 letters on their original finger (even if they change row or column). Only 2 letters change fingers on the same hand, and 2 change hands entirely.

Standard keyboard shortcuts (Ctrl + Z, X, C, V) do not move physically from their usual QWERTY positions.

The result is that you preserve **85% of your muscle memory** compared to QWERTY.

# Who is it for?
Practical users: For those who want to care for their hands with something simple, but cannot afford to spend months relearning a layout from scratch or lack the budget for expensive ergonomic hardware. It can be used on both ANSI and ISO keyboard formats.

Spanish speakers (and beyond): It is primarily optimized for Spanish. However, given its frequency-based architecture, it performs excellently in English, French, Portuguese, Italian, and other languages with Romance or Germanic roots.

# Why was it created?
It was born from a personal need. I am Venezuelan, writing is my hobby, and as a university student, I was tired of my fingers suffering from QWERTY's inefficiency.

Having no budget for an ortholinear or split keyboard, I looked for software alternatives (Dvorak, Colemak, Minimak). None satisfied me, as they are all Anglo-Saxon in origin; there was nothing designed for the flow of Spanish speakers. So, I spent 4 months working on it.

# How was it designed?
I deeply researched existing alternatives to extract the best from each. I analyzed the frequency of letters and bigrams in Spanish to distribute the most common keys to the strongest fingers. This allowed for a balanced workload, achieving a symmetry of 53% in the left hand and 47% in the right hand.

Furthermore, I studied hand biomechanics to prioritize the most comfortable movements (such as inward flexion or curl, and straight stretches instead of lateral ones). Typing on a flat keyboard is not a natural movement, but Arcadio ensures it is as smooth as possible without feeling confusing.

> Technical note: No automatic generation algorithm was used (I admit I didn't know how to install `Carpalx` on my PC!). Instead, the design was an empirical and artisanal process, using the Colemak-DH analyzer and the Keyboard Layout Analyzer (KLA) as guides to mathematically validate the efficiency of each iteration.
